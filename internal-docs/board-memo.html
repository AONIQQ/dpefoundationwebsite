Recommendation to the Board

Fundraising outreach: how we send, and what we build

A plan for reaching trustees, fraternity brothers, and the broader community, and a recommendation on the software behind it. Includes the alternatives we considered, two of which cost less.

Prepared for Al Grasso and Sanjay, Delta Phi Epsilon Foundation Prepared by Andrew Dorman, Aoniqq LLC Status For discussion

What we are recommending

Send the fundraising appeals in three waves, starting with the people closest to the Foundation. Build the list and sending tools into the admin dashboard the Foundation already owns and already knows how to use, rather than subscribing to a separate donor CRM. Keep Donorbox for donations, and convert Google Workspace to the free nonprofit edition.

The short version

A one-time build of roughly $4,000, after which the ongoing cost is about $20 per month. Two of the alternatives we reviewed cost less over five years. We are still recommending the build, for reasons stated plainly in the section on why.

The outreach sequence

Order matters. Each wave builds credibility for the next, and a strong result from the board is the single best predictor of how the wider appeals land.

  1. 1 Trustees The board first. Participation here is what allows the later appeals to say, honestly, that the people closest to the mission have already given.
  2. 2 Delta Phi Epsilon members, past and current The Alpha Chapter brotherhood, who already share the Foundation's purpose and need the least introduction to it.
  3. 3 Broader community Friends, former attendees, and others loosely associated with the Foundation. A warmer, more introductory message for people who may not know the full story.

Three designed email templates, one per audience, are drafted and attached separately. Each carries the Foundation crest, tells the story from 1962 forward, ties giving levels to real program costs ($250 Bleakley, $300 STIA, $500 Weiss and Butts), and includes the required unsubscribe and mailing address.

What Donorbox already covers, and what it does not

Donorbox is the right choice for donations and we are not proposing to replace any part of it. It is worth being precise about where it stops, because that boundary is the entire justification for building anything at all.

Why we recommend building anyway

Two options above cost less. The case for building is not that it saves money against every alternative, it is that the cheaper options carry a cost that does not appear on an invoice.

  • One system, not two. The Foundation's volunteers were trained on the admin dashboard and use it today to review scholarship applications and contact messages. Adding outreach there means no second login, no second tool, and no retraining as trustees turn over.
  • The realistic alternative is not a cheaper tool, it is an unused one. Subscription software that lives outside a volunteer's routine tends to go quiet, and the list goes stale with it. Tools that sit inside work already being done get used.
  • The Foundation owns the data and the software. Contacts live in the Foundation's own database, exportable at any time, with no per-contact pricing tier to climb as the list grows.
  • Vendor risk is real. Kindful, a well-known donor CRM, was absorbed into Bloomerang, and its pricing page now returns an error. Platforms are acquired, repriced, and retired. A system the Foundation owns is not subject to that.
The honest tradeoff

Custom software depends on the person who built it. That risk is mitigated with written documentation, a training session, and the code living in the Foundation's own repository on a conventional, widely used technology stack, but it should be named rather than glossed over.

A separate saving worth taking now

The Foundation currently pays for Google Workspace. As a recognized 501(c)(3) it very likely qualifies for Google Workspace for Nonprofits at no cost. Google's only exclusions are governmental bodies, hospitals, and schools. Fraternal and membership organizations are not excluded, and a separately incorporated educational foundation with its own 501(c)(3) is the standard qualifying structure.

Separately, the application requires an officer to attest that the Foundation does not discriminate in its programs. What would be built

A new section of the existing dashboard, in the same design the trustees already use.

  • Contacts. Import the Foundation's existing lists, with duplicate detection and a report of anything that could not be read. Search, edit, and tag by audience.
  • Segments. Save reusable groups such as trustees, members, and community, with a live count of who a message would reach.
  • Campaigns. Choose a segment, load one of the designed templates, preview it, send a test to yourself, then send.
  • Unsubscribe and compliance. One-click unsubscribe in every message, honored permanently and automatically, plus automatic removal of addresses that bounce or report spam.
  • Reporting. Per-campaign counts of delivered, bounced, and unsubscribed.
  • Email delivery setup. Domain authentication configured so mail reaches inboxes and the Foundation's everyday email is insulated from bulk sending.

Deliberately not included

Gift and pledge records, acknowledgment letter generation, event ticketing, and accounting integration are not part of this. Donorbox remains the system of record for donations. Keeping that boundary firm is what keeps this a modest project rather than a large one.

Investment

$4,000 one-time, fixed price

Includes:

  • Three designed and tested email templates, one per audience, already drafted
  • Email delivery and domain authentication setup
  • Contact database with import, segmentation, and export
  • Campaign composer and sending system
  • Unsubscribe, bounce, and complaint handling required by law
  • Delivery reporting
  • Written documentation and a live training session for the board
  • Ninety days of defect corrections after delivery

Estimated at about 35 hours of work at the Foundation's established rate, quoted as a fixed price so the Foundation is not exposed if it runs long. Ongoing cost after delivery is roughly $20 per month for email delivery, with no subscription or per-contact fees.

One variable

Consolidating the Foundation's existing contact records is the least predictable part. If the lists arrive as a single clean spreadsheet, it is covered above. If they arrive as several inconsistent sources, that additional cleanup would be quoted separately before any work begins, never added after the fact.

Next steps

  • Sanjay is gathering what contact data the Foundation holds: existing email lists, member rosters, and any records of past donors or interested friends. The completeness of those lists matters more to the result than anything on the software side.
  • The board reviews the three email templates and this recommendation.
  • Someone applies for Google for Nonprofits, which is free, takes a few days, and commits the Foundation to nothing.
  • On approval, work begins with contact import and domain setup, so the first appeal to trustees can go out promptly.
